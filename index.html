          How this was made

          The galaxies. Every point of light is one of the 12,710 galaxies of the IllustrisTNG simulation (TNG100) that the model learned from, followed from about 4.4 to 2.5 billion years ago. Their places in the sky are their mass of stars (left to right) and their gas for each star (bottom to top).

          The films. Every image comes from the particles of the simulation, rendered for this page. The simulation saves its state only every 110 to 240 million years (160 on average), so the frames in between are smooth blends of two real moments (the label says "blend"); nothing is invented between them. The slow camera turn is ours.

          The new films.

          Which galaxy plays. For each combination that exists in the simulation (32 of the 36) the film shows a typical real galaxy of that kind. Where more than 5 % of such galaxies switched off, a typical one of those plays as often as they really occur; otherwise a typical one that keeps forming stars plays.

          The model. The chance comes from a model that never saw this galaxy during training (cross-validation). It reads 17 numbers about the galaxy at the moment of your bet, not the images. It bets "switches off" when its chance is above 32 %.

          The opening.

          The suspense. During the future film the needle of the model's gauge swings for suspense; it stops on the model's real chance at the end. The last moments of the film are slowed down, except for the remarkable galaxies on the home page.

          The 3D view. Its time bar shows moments the simulation really saved; no particle is moved or invented. The camera's opening glide is ours; its distance is set from where the galaxy's stars are, so small and large galaxies fill the view alike.

          Data: IllustrisTNG project, TNG100-1, snapshots 72 to 84; your bet is placed 3.5 billion years ago.
